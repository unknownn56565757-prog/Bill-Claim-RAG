HOTEL ACCOMMODATION POLICY

Purpose
This policy outlines the rules for reimbursement of hotel accommodation expenses incurred during approved business travel.

Eligibility
Employees traveling on officially approved business trips are eligible for hotel reimbursement.

Approved Accommodation
Employees should book hotels that are reasonable, safe, and cost-effective.

Preferred hotel categories include:

• Standard Business Hotels
• Company-Approved Hotel Partners
• Hotels located near the business event or work site

Room Limits

Maximum reimbursable room rates per night:

• Domestic Travel: $150 per night
• International Travel: $250 per night

Higher rates require prior manager approval.

Reimbursable Expenses

The following hotel-related expenses are reimbursable:

• Room charges
• Mandatory taxes
• Service charges
• Internet charges required for business purposes
• Parking charges if business-related

Receipt Requirements

Employees must submit:

• Original hotel invoice
• Proof of payment
• Check-in and check-out dates
• Hotel name and address

Claims without valid receipts may be rejected.

Non-Reimbursable Expenses

The following expenses are not reimbursable:

• Mini-bar purchases
• Alcoholic beverages
• In-room movies and entertainment
• Spa services
• Personal laundry services
• Travel insurance purchased personally
• Room upgrades without approval

Extended Stay Rules

Employees should check out immediately after completion of business activities unless an extended stay has been approved.

Shared Accommodation

When practical, employees traveling together may be requested to share accommodation to reduce costs.

Cancellation Policy

Employees should cancel hotel reservations promptly when travel plans change to avoid unnecessary charges.

Approval Process

All hotel reimbursement claims require:

1. Submission of receipts.
2. Verification by Finance.
3. Manager approval.

Policy Violations

Providing false hotel receipts or claiming personal accommodation expenses may result in claim rejection and disciplinary action.

Examples

Example 1:
Employee stays at a hotel costing $120 per night during domestic travel.
Result: Reimbursable.

Example 2:
Employee books a luxury suite costing $500 per night without approval.
Result: May be rejected or partially reimbursed.

Example 3:
Employee claims minibar expenses of $40.
Result: Not reimbursable.

Example 4:
Employee stays an additional two days for personal reasons after the business trip ends.
Result: Personal stay expenses are not reimbursable.
